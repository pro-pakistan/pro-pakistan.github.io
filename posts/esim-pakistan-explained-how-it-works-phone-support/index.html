eSIM in Pakistan Explained: How It Works, Which Phones Support It, and What to Check Before Switching

An embedded SIM replaces the plastic card with a downloadable profile. Here is what that means for PTA registration, travel and dual-number use.

Zeeshan Ahmed Principal Telecom & Cloud Analyst
• 13 Oct 2026
1 CHIP MULTIPLE PROFILES

An eSIM is a small chip soldered inside a phone that can store a mobile operator profile downloaded over the air. It follows the GSMA remote SIM provisioning standard, which means the same hardware can hold profiles from different operators without swapping any plastic card.

For users in Pakistan the practical appeal is convenience: a lost or damaged SIM card is no longer a problem, a second number can live on the same phone alongside a physical SIM, and travellers can add a foreign data plan without removing their local line.

Availability depends on the operator and on the handset. Before switching, confirm that your operator currently offers eSIM activation, that your phone model supports eSIM, and that the device is properly registered with PTA.

PTA Registration Still Applies

eSIM does not bypass the Device Identification Registration and Blocking System. A phone that is not PTA-approved will lose network service whether it uses a physical SIM or an eSIM, so check the IMEI status through the official PTA DIRBS channels before buying an imported device.

How Activation Usually Works

Operators typically require biometric verification against your CNIC, after which they issue a QR code or activation code. Scanning it in the phone's mobile network settings downloads the profile. Keep the old physical SIM until the eSIM line is confirmed working.

Which Phones Support eSIM

Most recent flagship and many mid-range phones from Apple, Samsung and Google support eSIM, but regional variants differ. Check the settings menu for an option to add an eSIM, or look up the exact model number rather than the general model name.

⚡

Executive Takeaways & Key Insights

  • An eSIM stores operator profiles digitally on a chip inside the phone.
  • PTA device registration rules apply to eSIM exactly as they do to physical SIMs.
  • Confirm operator support, handset support and IMEI status before switching.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use an eSIM and a physical SIM together?

On most dual-SIM capable phones, yes. One line runs on the physical SIM and the second on the eSIM.

What happens to my eSIM if I change phones?

The profile does not move automatically. Ask your operator to reissue the eSIM for the new device, which usually involves verification again.
