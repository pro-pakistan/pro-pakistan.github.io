Raast's Silent Ascent: How Micro-Merchant QR Codes are Reshaping Pakistan's Cash Economy

The State Bank of Pakistan's instant payment rail, Raast, is quietly transforming everyday transactions across the nation. With over 40 million registered IDs and zero interchange fees, its P2M QR codes are empowering small businesses and pushing Pakistan towards a cashless future.

Fatima Noor Senior Tech & Telecom Editor
• 26 Sep 2026
PKR 1.2 Trillion Q2 FY24 TRANSACTIONS

Step into the bustling alleys of Lahore's Anarkali Bazaar, or navigate the crowded lanes near Karachi's I.I. Chundrigar Road, and the rhythm of commerce is almost exclusively punctuated by the rustle of banknotes. For decades, the ubiquitous 'kiryana' store, the street-side chai-wala, and the small electronics repair shop in Hafeez Center have operated firmly in a cash-first ecosystem. A customer buying a pack of biscuits for PKR 150 or paying PKR 500 for a phone cover would invariably hand over physical cash, a practice deeply ingrained in Pakistan's economic fabric, despite intermittent pushes for digitization.

However, a quiet transformation is underway, largely spearheaded by the State Bank of Pakistan’s (SBP) instant payment system, Raast. Launched with a vision to catalyze financial inclusion and digitize payments, Raast has swiftly scaled to become a formidable force. With over 40 million unique identifiers registered and transaction volumes soaring, its Person-to-Merchant (P2M) feature, powered by simple QR codes, is democratizing digital payments, bringing formal financial channels to the grassroots level of the economy, particularly the micro-merchants.

The challenge for any digital payment initiative in Pakistan has always been multifaceted. Deep-seated cultural reliance on cash, coupled with varying levels of digital literacy and access to banking services, presents a formidable barrier. Previous attempts, while making inroads, often struggled with scalability among smaller vendors due to prohibitive transaction fees, complex onboarding processes, or the sheer cost of Point-of-Sale (POS) infrastructure. Raast aims to circumvent these traditional hurdles by offering a streamlined, cost-effective alternative.

This initiative specifically targets the backbone of Pakistan's retail landscape: the millions of small, independent businesses that collectively form a significant portion of the informal economy. By enabling these micro-merchants to accept digital payments instantly, securely, and most importantly, without any transaction fees for either party, Raast is not just offering a payment solution; it's fostering an ecosystem shift. It's about empowering the vendor who previously relied solely on a cash drawer, offering them a gateway to formal banking and its associated benefits, one QR code scan at a time.

The Mechanics of a Digital Revolution: How Raast P2M Works

At its core, Raast P2M is elegant in its simplicity. Merchants, regardless of their scale, can generate a unique QR code directly from their bank's mobile application or through their bank's branch. This QR code, whether displayed as a digital image on a phone or a printed sticker on the counter, acts as a universal payment identifier. Customers simply open their banking app, scan the Raast QR code, enter the amount, and confirm the transaction with their PIN or biometrics. The payment is instantly credited to the merchant's bank account, often with an SMS notification, providing real-time confirmation.

This system bypasses the need for expensive dedicated POS terminals, which can cost small businesses upwards of PKR 15,000 for hardware alone, not to mention recurring maintenance and connectivity charges. Unlike traditional card-based payments that involve multiple intermediaries and associated interchange fees (often 1.5% to 2.5% per transaction), Raast P2M transactions incur zero charges for both the customer and the merchant. This zero-fee model, mandated by SBP circulars, is Raast's most compelling proposition, significantly lowering the barrier to entry for digitization for millions of micro-merchants previously excluded by cost.

Bridging the Digital Divide: Challenges and Opportunities

Despite its inherent advantages, Raast P2M faces significant challenges in a country like Pakistan. Digital literacy, particularly outside major urban centers like Islamabad's Blue Area, remains a key hurdle. Many small shopkeepers or their customers may not be comfortable with smartphone banking apps or trust digital transactions over tangible cash. Furthermore, while smartphone penetration is growing, the affordability of higher-end devices and consistent, reliable internet access from local ISPs like PTCL or StormFiber can still be an issue. SBP and partner banks are actively working on awareness campaigns, simplifying user interfaces, and providing multi-lingual support to address these gaps.

Another critical factor is the cost of smartphones themselves, often exacerbated by PTA DIRBS IMEI registration slabs and associated taxes. A significant portion of the population relies on basic or older smartphones that might struggle with modern banking applications. However, the rapidly declining cost of entry-level smartphones, coupled with increasing data plan affordability from telcos like Jazz, Telenor, and Zong, presents a growing opportunity. As more Pakistanis gain access to capable devices and reliable internet, the potential user base for Raast will naturally expand, making digital payments a more viable and ubiquitous option.

Economic Reshaping: Financial Inclusion and Formalization

The true long-term impact of Raast P2M extends beyond simple transactions; it's about fundamentally reshaping Pakistan's informal economy. By enabling micro-merchants to accept digital payments, Raast inherently brings them into the formal financial system. Every Raast transaction leaves a digital footprint, providing a verifiable record of sales. For merchants, this means a history of financial activity, which can be invaluable for accessing formal credit facilities from banks – a critical stepping stone for business growth that was previously unavailable to those operating purely in cash. This formalization also reduces the risks associated with handling large amounts of physical cash, from theft to counterfeit currency.

Moreover, the comprehensive data generated by Raast transactions could, in the future, offer FBR valuable insights into economic activity, potentially broadening the tax base and improving revenue collection through fair taxation, though currently Raast transactions are not directly linked to tax liabilities for small merchants in a punitive way. The zero-fee model is crucial here; it avoids creating a disincentive for formalization, which has plagued past initiatives. Executive Takeaways & Key Insights

  • Raast's zero-fee model is its greatest strength, democratizing digital payments by removing cost barriers for micro-merchants and customers.
  • Successful adoption requires continued efforts in digital literacy, trust-building, and ensuring smartphone and internet accessibility nationwide.
  • The P2M QR system bypasses expensive traditional POS infrastructure, making digital payments feasible for even the smallest businesses.
  • Formalizing transactions via Raast provides merchants with digital financial records, essential for accessing credit and fostering economic inclusion.

Frequently Asked Questions

How secure are Raast P2M transactions for both customers and merchants?

Raast transactions are highly secure, leveraging SBP's robust infrastructure and bank-level encryption. Customers authorize payments via their banking app using biometrics or PINs, ensuring authenticity. Merchants receive instant notifications directly into their linked bank accounts, minimizing fraud risks associated with cash and providing an undeniable digital record of payment.

What are the basic requirements for a small shopkeeper to start accepting Raast payments?

A small shopkeeper only needs a valid CNIC, a smartphone (though not always required for receiving payments, only for managing), and an account with any SBP-licensed commercial bank. They can then generate a static Raast QR code through their bank's mobile application or by visiting a branch, displaying it for customer scans to accept instant digital payments.

How does Raast P2M contribute to Pakistan's broader financial inclusion goals?

By making digital payments accessible and free for even the smallest vendors, Raast brings previously unbanked or underbanked micro-merchants into the formal financial system. This provides them with a digital transaction history, potentially enabling access to credit, insurance, and other financial services, thereby fostering economic growth and reducing reliance on the informal economy.
